The game

Two players, Black and White, take turns placing one stone on any empty cell. Black goes first. Stones never move. They stay where they are put unless they are captured and taken off the board.

The player with more points at the end wins. You get a point for every empty cell you have surrounded and a point for every stone you have captured. White also gets extra points for moving second, called komi. Komi is currently 6, similar to Go.

The board is a hexagon, and its size is the number of cells along one side: 5 per side has 61 cells, 7 per side has 127.

Groups

Stones of one colour that touch each other form a group. A group is captured or survives as a whole, so joining your stones together is how you make them strong.

Liberty pairs

An empty cell next to a group is one of its liberties. In HexGo one liberty is not enough. A group needs a liberty pair: two empty cells that touch each other and both touch the group.

After every move, a group with no liberty pair is captured and removed, unless it has eyes. Eyes come later.

Edges and corners

A stone on the edge has fewer neighbours, so it has fewer ways to make a pair. Two stones are enough to capture a stone on the edge. One stone can capture a stone in the corner.

Eyes

An eye is an empty area closed in by a single group: every stone around it belongs to that group. The empty cells inside are its eye cells, and they are counted across all of the group's eyes.

A group with at least two eye cells, a pair of eyes, stays on the board even when it has no liberty pair. The shape of its eyes decides how safe it is.

Eyes on the edge

The edge of the board works as a wall. An area closed in by your stones and the edge is an eye, the same as one ringed completely by stones.

That makes eyes much cheaper at the edge. Five stones hold three eye cells there. In the middle of the board it takes ten.

The triangle of death

Three eye cells that all touch each other look like the safest eye of the three-cell shapes. They are the only one that dies. A stone played inside a line of three has no liberty pair, so it is not allowed. A stone played inside a triangle touches the two other cells, and those two touch each other. That is a pair, so the move is legal.

White does no better by leaving the first black stone alone. Black adds a second stone inside, White is down to one eye cell, and the whole group is captured. Once a group surrounded on the outside depends on a triangle, it cannot be saved, so give your eye a different shape while you still can.

Who is captured first

After a stone is placed, the board is checked in a fixed order.

  1. The opponent's groups are checked. Any with no liberty pair and no pair of eyes are removed.
  2. Then the mover's own groups are checked. If one of them would be captured, the move is suicide and is not allowed.

So a stone that looks doomed is legal when it captures something first. One more detail: a stone played inside an eye that cannot survive there does not break the eye open. The cell it sits on stops counting, but the rest of the eye still does.

No repeats

A move may not bring back an arrangement of the whole board that has already happened in the game. This stops two players capturing back and forth for ever. Go players call the situation ko.

Ending and scoring

When you see nothing useful left to play, pass. Two passes in a row end the game. Then the score is counted.

  1. Stones that could not avoid capture are taken off as dead. They count as captures for the other player.
  2. Each player gets one point for every empty cell in an area bordered only by their own stones, and one point for every stone captured.
  3. White adds 6 points of komi for moving second.

In computer test games with no komi, Black finished about 5 points ahead on boards of 5 and 6 per side and about 8 ahead on 4 per side. Komi is set at 6 for now, similar to Go. Because it is a whole number, a game can end in a draw.
